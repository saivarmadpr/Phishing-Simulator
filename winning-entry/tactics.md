# Tactics Used in the Phishing Email

## 1. Scenario
- ### Scenario for the Phishing Email:

**Scenario Title**: "Delivery Issue Notification – Address and Payment Confirmation"

**Context**:  
The recipient receives an email claiming to be from USPS, alerting them to a problem with the delivery of a package. The email employs urgency and fear of inconvenience to compel the recipient to act without scrutiny.

**Key Details of the Email**:
1. **Subject Line**: "Action Required: Delivery Issue Detected – Update Your Address and Payment Method Now!!!"  
   - Designed to grab attention and create urgency.
   
2. **Body Content**:
   - A tracking number is included to give the email a sense of legitimacy.
   - The recipient is informed that their package delivery is delayed due to an "address issue."
   - A fake timeline is imposed, stating that the package will be returned and a fee of $100 charged unless the recipient updates their address and payment information within 48 hours.

3. **Call-to-Action Links**:
   - **Fake Tracking Link**: Redirects to a malicious website disguised as a USPS page to collect sensitive information.
   - **Malicious Attachment**: Links to a fake invoice file (`invoice.zip`), which may contain malware.

4. **Social Engineering Techniques**:
   - **Urgency**: Deadline of 48 hours to update the information.
   - **Fear**: Warning of package return and a $100 charge.
   - **Authority**: Use of USPS branding and links to official USPS social media accounts.

5. **Red Flags for Awareness**:
   - Generic greeting ("dear customer") instead of a personalized name.
   - Grammatical and spelling errors (e.g., "Due to a issue").
   - Links pointing to non-USPS domains (e.g., `uspssmail.com`).
   - Unexpected attachment (`invoice.zip`).

---

## 2. Phishing Techniques
1. **Urgent Language**: Created a sense of urgency to compel the recipient to act quickly.
2. **Impersonation**: Mimicked a trusted organization using realistic branding.
3. **Fake Links**: Included links pointing to a fake website.

## 3. Educational Value
- Highlighted red flags such as:
  1. Mismatched domain names.
  2. Generic greetings like "Dear User."
  3. Poor grammar or spelling mistakes.
